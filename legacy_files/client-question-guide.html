How to use this guide

Start with your business purpose, then move into audience, content, design, and practical setup. You do not need perfect answers. Short, honest answers are enough to shape the first version of your website.

Critical questions are the ones that affect launch. Recommended questions improve the quality of the site. Optional questions help when the project is more brand-sensitive or likely to grow later.

1. Clarify What problem is the website solving?
2. Prove What evidence will make visitors trust you?
3. Decide What must be editable after launch?

What exact business name should appear on the website?

Write the public trading name, and include the legal name if it is different. Example: "Bayview Corp" as the brand, but "Bayview Corp Pty Ltd" in legal or footer details.

Critical

In one sentence, what does the business do?

Use plain language. Example: "We help landowners and buyers through boutique residential property developments."

Critical

What should people believe after 30 seconds on the site?

Examples: "We are trustworthy", "we are premium", "we know this market", or "we are easy to contact".

Recommended

What makes this business different from nearby competitors?

Think about genuine differences: niche, location, experience, service style, process, quality, speed, price, or personal involvement.

Recommended
This section keeps the website from sounding generic. A premium site still needs a clear business sentence before it needs visual polish.

Who is the most important visitor for version one?

Choose one primary group if you can. Examples: buyers, landowners, investors, referrers, families, local businesses, or hiring candidates.

Critical

What question is that visitor trying to answer?

Examples: "Can I trust this business?", "Do you serve my area?", "Have you done this before?", or "How do I contact you?"

Critical

What action should the visitor take before they leave?

Examples: call, send an enquiry, book a consultation, view projects, download a brochure, or register interest.

Recommended

What proof can your website show to build trust?

Examples: completed work, years of experience, testimonials, client logos, project photos, awards, qualifications, media, certifications, or partner names.

Critical

Which photos, videos, documents, or logos are approved to use?

Only list items you are comfortable publishing. Images, testimonials, partner logos, and project details should be approved before they go on the website.

Critical

Who will approve the final wording before launch?

This matters when claims involve experience, results, pricing, availability, legal details, finance, or health and safety.

Recommended

Do you need a content update process after launch?

Example: the website team updates it for you monthly, or you use a private editing screen for projects, news, services, or team members.

Optional

Which pages are needed for launch?

Examples: Home, About, Services, Projects, Contact, FAQ, Careers, Downloads, or a single scrolling page with sections.

Critical

What can wait until version two?

Examples: full gallery, blog, newsletter, portal, filters, downloads, multilingual content, or a client login area.

Recommended

Which parts do you need to edit yourself?

Common editable areas are projects, news, testimonials, and team profiles. You do not need editing access for every sentence on the site.

Recommended

Which reference websites should we look at, and why?

Write what you like specifically: photography, layout, luxury feel, simplicity, navigation, colours, or wording.

Critical

What should the website never feel like?

Examples: cheap, too corporate, too flashy, too animated, too text-heavy, hard to read, slow, or generic.

Recommended

Do you already have brand colours, fonts, or a logo?

If not, it is useful to decide whether you need a simple temporary visual direction or a full brand identity.

Optional

Who will own the domain and main website accounts?

Your business should own its domain and main accounts. The website team can help set them up, but the ownership details should stay with the business.

Critical

Where should contact form submissions go?

Write the exact email address, who monitors it, and whether you need a saved copy for your records.

Critical

How often will the website need updates?

Examples: rarely, monthly, after each new project, or when a target market changes. This decides whether you need a private editing screen.

Recommended

What needs to be backed up or exported?

Examples: contact form records, project details, uploaded images, or a monthly website backup.

Optional

Decision framework

Use this section to understand which kind of website setup matches the way your business actually needs to manage content.

Simple website

Best when most pages rarely change and you want speed, simplicity, and low maintenance.

Website with editable sections

Best when one content area changes regularly, such as projects, news, or team profiles.

Full business system

Only needed when the website itself must manage leads, users, reminders, private files, or internal workflows.

Need Recommended answer Why it matters
Editable projects Private project editor You can add, edit, hide, and delete project entries.
Contact enquiries Contact form with email notification You receive enquiries without needing to manage extra systems.
Custom domain Business-owned domain Keeps the site fast while your business owns the domain.
